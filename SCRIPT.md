**Facts:** 
- In WWII, the British planned to build an aircraft carrier out of 'Pykrete' (ice + wood pulp) to solve the mid-Atlantic air-gap.
- Pykrete is 14% wood pulp, 86% ice. It is much tougher and melts slower than pure ice.
- Project Habakkuk never saw combat, but prototypes were built.

**Script (approx 83s):**
What if I told you the British once planned to build an aircraft carrier out of ice during World War 2? 
German submarines were sinking Allied ships across the Atlantic. 
And there was a huge stretch where planes couldn't provide enough protection. 
So one British inventor had a completely insane idea: build a gigantic floating airfield made from ice! 
But they didn't plan to use ordinary ice. 
It was made by mixing roughly 14% wood pulp with 86% water. 
The result was stronger than normal ice and melted much slower. 
It was called Pykrete.
They proposed an aircraft carrier 2,000 feet long. 
Luckily, conventional carriers improved before they needed a floating popsicle. 
Unluckily, the secret ingredient—wood pulp—is just paper, which happens to be flammable. 
Luckily, it works like a giant thermal insulator.
A carrier made of paper and frozen water. 
Now that is a cool way to win a war!
